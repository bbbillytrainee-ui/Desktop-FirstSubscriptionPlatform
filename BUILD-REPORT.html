Frontend

The reader experience

Publication

  • Home page as a magazine: cover, latest dossiers with a live news feed, a swipeable "more from this issue" row, last month's most-read, Core Coverage, the next webinar and the member network, research briefings, and membership.
  • Article reader: table of contents, adjustable text size and a focus mode, references, and "Next up" plus related dossiers at the end.
  • 3D magazine edition: each monthly issue opens as a page-turning flipbook, with an archive of past issues.
  • Finding things: topic filters across 7 coverage areas, and site-wide search (Ctrl+K).
  • 25+ sections: including webinars, events, podcasts, videos, reports, trends, the regulatory navigator, vendors, subscriptions and advertising.

Reasons to stay and come back

  • Continue reading: returning readers see their unfinished article at the top, with how much is left.
  • Saved articles: bookmark any dossier and find it again in a Saved panel.
  • Next up and related: the end of every article leads to the next relevant read.

Design quality

  • Core Coverage carousel: advances on its own, and readers stay in control (it pauses on hover, and has a play/pause button, clickable steps and keyboard support).
  • Visual polish: fanned issue cards, subtle scroll reveals, a chapter rail for navigating the page, and light and dark themes.
  • Accessibility: keyboard navigation throughout, a skip link, screen-reader labels, AA colour contrast checked in both themes, and motion that switches off for readers who prefer less.

Connected to the backend (new this week)

  • Real sign-in: create an account or sign in. The session survives a page reload and signs itself back in securely.
  • Real paywall: Professional articles show a preview when signed out or on the free plan, and unlock for Professional members.

Backend

The platform behind it

Accounts and security

  • Registration, sign-in, sign-out, email verification and password reset, with reset links that expire after an hour and work once.
  • Passwords hashed with Argon2, the current industry recommendation. A failed sign-in never reveals whether an email has an account.
  • Short-lived sessions (15-minute access tokens) renewed through a secure cookie that page scripts can't read. A stolen, reused session token signs that login out everywhere.
  • Safe by default in production: the server refuses to start with placeholder secrets, open cross-site access or insecure cookies.

Content

  • Dossiers: newest first, filtered by topic or issue, with full-text search that ranks title matches above body matches and understands word forms ("manufacture" finds "manufacturing").
  • Related dossiers, topic counts and the monthly issues, each with its editorial column and key signals.
  • Server-enforced paywall: free and signed-out readers receive only the preview. The rest is never sent to their browser.
  • Every list is paginated, so no request can pull the whole database.

Reliability

  • 70 automated tests against a real PostgreSQL database cover sign-in, token abuse, the paywall, search and the content import.
  • Database changes are versioned (Alembic migrations), reviewable and reversible, and are tested on every run.
  • Content import: the existing site content was migrated into the database, cleaned of any HTML that could carry scripts, and checked by a round-trip test that it comes back identical.
